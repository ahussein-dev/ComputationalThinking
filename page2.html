Famous Computer Scientist

Biography

Joan Clarke study Mathematics and awarded a double first in 1940. Her supervisor persuaded her to come to Bletchley Park, after the completion of her studies. When Clarke initially joined Bletchley, she was assigned clerical work and paid significantly less that her male counterparts.

However, within a few days, her abilities shone through and she started work in Hut 8. She worked alongside the famous Alan Turning. Clarke was the only woman on this team where the Enigma code was broken. The ciphers that Clarke and her colleagues broke was the most complex that Bletchley Park had worked on. The results of Clarke's work resulted in immediate military action on U-boats. Clarke's contribution was the second stage of the procedure. It took the Stecker of the letter E and assumed that all plaintext consisted of the letter E. From this they were able to reduce the amount of shipping sunk from 282,00 tons to 62,000 tons per month. Her work helped shorten the war and save many lives on both sides of the conflict.

Clarke was appointed MBE in 1947 for her work during WW2, but Clarke's work on the Enigma project was rarely acknowledged, along with other female codebreakers that worked at Bletchley. In addition to this her work is significantly overshadowed by her relationship to Alan Turing. Articles and biographies of Clarke tend to focus on her engagement rather than her work. Not only has she inspired women within the computer science and tech field, but she has also inspired women to join male dominated fields such as engineering. She inspires women to believe they are capable of achieving higher than expected just like she did. Her work still impacts the world we live in today as the Chief Security Officer, Sunila Shivpuri said “While Joan Clarke used her codebreaking skills to help defend her country during wartime, codebreakers in the present day sometimes pursue more criminal objectives.”[10]
